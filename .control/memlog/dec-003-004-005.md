# Memlog — DEC-003, DEC-004, DEC-005

## Who accepted, and on what authority

`wdi-decision` intent `accept` is the Product Owner's act alone, and an agent MUST NOT accept its
own decision. It did not here. The repo owner, on 2026-08-23, authorised every gate in this pass in
writing and in advance — *"masalah soal gate, saya setujui semuanya. nanti saya review hasil akhir"*
— and asked for the whole run to proceed without further questions, reviewing the finished result.

That is the same standing authorisation already recorded against `gates_passed` in
`.control/registry/index.yaml`. It is written down here rather than assumed, because the rule it
stands in for exists to stop an agent clearing its own way forward unasked. The owner's review of
the result is where the acceptance is actually tested.

Before the authorisation was given, three forks were put to the owner and answered directly:
process decomposition, corpus depth, and the shape of the Quality Budget control. All three DECs
record the answer the owner chose, not the agent's preference standing in for one.

## What prompted them

The owner's first sustained look at the shipped r1/r2 product. Their report was a list of experience
defects, not missing capability:

- The running window could not be identified as Snapdown → DEC-003.
- The Editor could not be found; only Settings appeared → traced to a stale `desktop.exe` in
  `target/release/` beside the correct `Snapdown.exe`, both present, the stale one older than the
  rename commit `4c5c901`. The stale artifacts were removed. The tab navigation, the Vault Browse
  button, and the hotkey recorder all exist in the current build and were confirmed reachable by the
  UI audit worker.
- Hotkey labels unreadable against their background → a real defect, held for `wdi-ux`; root cause is
  light-only hard-coded colours in JSX mixed with tokens that follow `prefers-color-scheme`.
- Settings longer than its four choices warrant → real, and held for `wdi-ux`.
- Raw long-edge and encoder-quality numbers, against a TinyPNG expectation → DEC-004.
- Desktop first, publishing second, MCP want unformed → DEC-005.

## What was deliberately not decided

- **The MCP shape.** The owner said they do not know what they want from it. That is an open
  question for `wdi-question`, not a decision. DEC-005 holds `agent-access` still *because* the want
  is unformed, and says so.
- **The experience bar itself.** DEC-005 names a condition — "until the experience bar set at G2 is
  met" — that does not yet exist. Its Cost section admits this. `wdi-ux` has to produce it before
  DEC-005 can be checked by anyone.
- **OQ-3.** DEC-004 restates it rather than closing it. The question stops being "what is the right
  constant" and becomes "is the derivation legible at its smallest output." Still unmeasured.

## Reasoning that did not survive into the DEC text

Snagit was the owner's stated benchmark and Snagit splits into two processes. DEC-003 goes the other
way. The reason that split is not evidence: it is a consequence of Snagit's age and its C++/WPF
lineage, and every constraint that forced it — no tray-owning framework, no cheap transparent
overlay, no shared in-process window lifecycle — Tauri v2 removes. Copying the split would have been
copying a workaround for a problem this product does not have. What the owner actually wanted from
the comparison was the *naming*, and that is what DEC-003 gives them.

## 2026-08-23 — intent `apply`, and one step that had nothing to do

**event** — Raised `DEC-003`, `DEC-004` and `DEC-005` from `accepted` to `applied`, with `touches`
filled from the corpus files that actually cite each: 10, 17 and 11 respectively.

**event** — Found by `wdi-reconcile`, finding **D1** of `RECONCILE-2026-08-23`. The three sat at
`accepted` with `touches: []` while their content had already been carried into roughly thirty corpus
files. The registry said *not yet applied*; reality said applied everywhere. `V8` cannot see this — it
checks that an `applied` decision names a non-empty `touches`, not that an `accepted` one which has
plainly been applied was ever raised.

**decision** — Step 2 of intent `apply`, *dispatch the owner of every document the decision reaches*,
**had nothing to do, and that is stated rather than skipped.** Every target had already been written
by its own owner while the gates ran: `wdi-problem` wrote the brief, `wdi-product` the PRD,
`wdi-blueprint` the glossary and spine and C4 and business rules, `wdi-component` the SDDs and their
slots, `wdi-ux` the `DESIGN` and `EXPERIENCE` documents. Re-dispatching them to write what they had
already written would have produced no change and a false trail.

**note** — The honest reading of how the gap opened: the *work* of applying was done continuously, by
the right owners, as each gate ran. The *act* of recording that it had been done was never performed,
because nothing in a gate skill's own flow reaches back to raise a `DEC-`. That is worth remembering —
it will happen again on the next decision that is carried by the gates rather than applied on its own.

**event** — `touches` was built from `git grep` over `.what/`, `.how/` and `.control/`, excluding
working output (`_bmad-output/`), the question lists, the registry itself, and the generated folder.
A citation is the evidence a file was reached; a file that changed for this decision and cites nothing
would be invisible to that method, and none was found.

**note** — From this point the three files MUST NOT be edited. Applying is what freezes a decision,
and roughly thirty documents now cite them.
